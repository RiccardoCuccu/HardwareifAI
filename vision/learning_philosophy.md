# Learning Philosophy

The manifesto states what belongs in this curriculum and why. This page states how learning actually happens while working through it.

## Self-review is sufficient

Every milestone closes on self-review by default: the learner's own honest assessment against the same technical bar an external reviewer would apply is enough, on its own, to call a module done. External feedback, if it ever arrives through the project's public channels, is a genuinely welcome bonus that adds confidence beyond that baseline, but it is never solicited on a deadline, never waited for, and its total absence never blocks, weakens, or delays anything. A curriculum designed this way has to work end to end with zero external participation, because that is the realistic default, not the exception.

## Learn by building, escalating in ambition

The manifesto's implementation-over-memorisation principle sets the rule that every module ships with a project. In practice, that plays out as an escalating ladder: early projects are small, single-purpose hardware units, verified standalone in simulation; later projects compound what came before into larger, multi-block subsystems as skill actually accumulates. Each step is checked against its own test suite and a stated performance target before the next one starts, so ambition grows at the pace competence does, not ahead of it.

## Tools, just-in-time

Toolchain pieces are installed and learned only when a specific artifact actually needs them, never set up in a batch upfront on the assumption they'll eventually be useful. The same applies to supporting skills more broadly: they get picked up just before the step that requires them, which keeps early momentum from being spent on setup instead of on building something real.

## Two speeds of knowledge

Foundations, established architectures, systems, and optimisation techniques form a stable layer, revisited infrequently because they don't need to change often. Newer models and techniques sit in a separate emerging layer, tracked at thin awareness only: watched, noted, never given a dedicated deep-dive module until they prove durable enough to earn one. Treating these as two different maintenance speeds, rather than one undifferentiated pile of "things to keep up with," is what keeps the curriculum current without constantly rewriting its core.
