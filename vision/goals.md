# Goals

## Learner goals

By the end of this curriculum, the learner should be able to:

- Design AI accelerators, from individual RTL blocks up through full inference and training pipelines.
- Understand what modern models actually need from hardware, across model families, not just the language models that dominate public attention.
- Collaborate effectively with ML researchers, sharing enough common ground to bridge the hardware/software gap in both directions.
- Reason clearly about hardware/software trade-offs instead of treating either side as a black box.
- Read a paper and translate what it describes into a concrete hardware requirement.
- Optimise both inference and training workloads, not just one of the two.
- Track where the field is heading without chasing every new trend as if it were already established.

## Project goals

Beyond any individual learner's outcomes, the curriculum itself is designed to reach these project-level goals:

- A portfolio of independently verified SystemVerilog hardware artifacts, solid enough to hold up under real technical-interview scrutiny.
- A living curriculum that is never declared "finished," where every included or excluded topic carries a written, auditable rationale.
- Full coverage of the modern AI taxonomy relevant to hardware design, so the course does not quietly narrow down to language models alone.
- A project that works end to end on self-directed effort alone, at zero operating cost, with external participation welcome but never required for any of the above to hold.
