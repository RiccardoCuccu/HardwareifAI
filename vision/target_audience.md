# Target Audience

This curriculum is for anyone with a foundation in electronic engineering or digital design, whether that foundation is a master's degree just finished or years of professional RTL work, who wants to specialise in one specific direction: AI-accelerator hardware design.

The target sub-role is AI-accelerator RTL and microarchitecture design specifically. Every project in this curriculum is built to prove that exact competence, whichever door the reader walked in through.

Because readers arrive at different starting points, the curriculum treats foundational RTL, SystemVerilog, and verification concepts as a fast-review layer, not a first-principles course. Readers who already work in digital design move through that layer quickly and spend most of their time on the AI-accelerator-specific material that follows; readers newer to hardware get the same refresher at the pace they actually need.

What every reader builds from scratch, regardless of where they start, is the one layer this curriculum actually exists to teach: how modern AI workloads, across model families and not just large language models, translate into concrete hardware requirements.

That target learner needs a 360-degree view of the field: hardware first, with enough software awareness to understand how the workloads they're designing for actually run, kept current against the scientific literature rather than frozen at whatever was true when they started.

"360-degree" does not mean shallow. The curriculum favours the field's main, established research lines over speculative or likely short-lived branches, giving promising-but-unproven areas a brief mention rather than a deep dive, until they've earned a fuller module.

This curriculum is also written from a specific vantage point: someone who already thinks in RTL, microarchitecture, and verification, moving from general digital design into AI-accelerator hardware. That vantage point is one instance of the practising-engineer path described above, not a separate case: it shapes which explanations get full depth (the AI-accelerator-specific layer) and which get treated as a fast refresher (general RTL and verification), the same split any reader with a digital-design background experiences.
